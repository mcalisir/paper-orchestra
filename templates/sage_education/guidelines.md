# Manuscript guidelines: empirical education article (Sage / Taylor & Francis)

## Target
* Peer-reviewed, SSCI/Scopus-indexed education journal.
* Follow the target journal's own author guidelines when they differ from this file.

## Length
* Main text: **7,000-9,000 words**, excluding the abstract, tables and references.
* Abstract: one paragraph, **150-250 words**. State aim, design, participants, instruments, main results with key statistics, and implication.
* Keywords: **5 keywords**.
* Suggested share of the main text: Introduction 8-10%, Theoretical Framework 25-30%, Methodology 15-20%, Results 15-20%, Discussion 25-30%, Conclusion 3-5%.

## Required structure (use these headings)
1. Introduction: problem, gap, purpose, significance, and numbered research questions at the end.
2. Theoretical Framework: thematic subsections that build the argument and lead to the research questions.
3. Methodology: Research Design, Participants, Instruments, Procedure, Data Analysis.
4. Results: one subsection per research question, titled "RQ1: ...".
5. Discussion: Overview of Findings, thematic subsections interpreting results against the literature, Limitations.
6. Conclusion: answer the purpose and give practical and research implications that the evidence supports.
7. Declarations and References.

## Reporting (APA 7 and APA JARS)
* Report sample size, selection, demographics and setting.
* Report each instrument's source, structure, scoring, and validity and reliability evidence **for this sample**.
* Report the statistical test, statistic, degrees of freedom, exact p value, effect size and confidence interval when they are available.
* Italicize statistical symbols. Omit the leading zero for values that cannot exceed 1 (p, r, alpha).
* Tables follow APA style, using `booktabs` with no vertical rules.

## Citations
* APA 7 through biblatex. Use `\citet{key}` for narrative citations and `\citep{key}` for parenthetical citations.

## Anonymity
* Double-blind. Do not include author names, institutions, city or university names, grant numbers, or identifying self-citations.
